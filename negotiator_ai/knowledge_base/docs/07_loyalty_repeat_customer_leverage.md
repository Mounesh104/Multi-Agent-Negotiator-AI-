# Loyalty & Repeat-Customer Leverage Tactics

## The Repeat-Customer Advantage
Repeat customers are significantly more valuable to suppliers than first-time buyers:
- **Customer acquisition cost (CAC):** Acquiring a new B2B customer costs 5–7× more than retaining an existing one.
- **Payment reliability:** Repeat customers have established payment track records — lower credit risk for the supplier.
- **Order predictability:** Regular buyers smooth the supplier's production and inventory planning.
- **Referral value:** Satisfied repeat buyers are a free marketing channel for the supplier.

Smart negotiators make these economics visible to the supplier — don't assume they already appreciate your value.

## Core Loyalty Leverage Tactics

### 1. Quantify Your Relationship Value
Before any negotiation, calculate and state your cumulative spend:
> "Over the past [18 months], we've placed [X] orders totaling approximately [$Y] with your company. That's a reliable, growing revenue stream for you."

Always use specific numbers. Vague references ("we've been buying from you for a while") carry no weight.

### 2. The Lifetime Value Framing
Project your future spend based on your growth plan:
> "Based on our current growth trajectory, we expect to increase our orders by 20–30% over the next 12 months. That means you're looking at a relationship worth approximately [$Z] annually — and we'd like our pricing to reflect that partnership."

### 3. Payment History as Leverage
If you have a perfect or near-perfect on-time payment record, cite it explicitly:
> "We've paid every invoice within terms for [X months/years] — not once late. That's a credit risk profile your finance team values. We'd like that reliability to be reflected in our pricing."

### 4. The "Tenure Discount" Request
Frame the ask as a standard business practice, not a favor:
> "Many of your established customers likely receive a loyalty or tenure pricing tier. We'd like to understand how we can qualify for that — or work with you to create it."

### 5. Incremental Volume Offer
Combine loyalty leverage with a volume commitment:
> "As a thank-you for the loyalty discount, we're prepared to increase our monthly commitment from [X] to [Y] units and set up a standing order. That gives you the demand predictability of a new large account, with the payment reliability of an established relationship."

## Supplier Relationship Milestones (When Leverage Increases)
| Milestone | Leverage Gained |
|---|---|
| 3rd order placed | Established relationship; ask for Net 30 terms |
| 6-month mark | Request 3–5% loyalty discount; ask for dedicated account rep |
| 12-month mark | Request Net 60 terms; ask for annual price lock |
| 2+ years | Request preferred-supplier agreement; ask for access to new products at pre-launch prices |
| >$50K cumulative spend | Request VIP pricing tier; request executive-level relationship management |

## The "We've Never Asked for a Discount" Opener
For long-term relationships where you've never actively negotiated:
> "We've been with you for [X time] and have honestly never pushed hard on pricing. We've appreciated the service quality. But as we grow, we need to make sure our cost structure is competitive. Can we talk about what a long-term partner rate might look like?"

This acknowledges the relationship without aggression and signals this is a new, ongoing dynamic.

## Handling the "We Treat All Customers Equally" Response
> "We understand you have standard pricing — we're not asking for special treatment. We're asking for the pricing that reflects our order history, payment reliability, and projected volume growth. That's a different conversation than discounting for a new account."

## Key Benchmarks
- Average loyalty/tenure discount achievable after 12 months: **3–7%**
- Payment history premium (for perfect record): **1–3% additional discount**
- Loyalty discount achievable by explicitly citing cumulative spend: **5–10%** (most suppliers have never been asked to calculate this for a small buyer)
