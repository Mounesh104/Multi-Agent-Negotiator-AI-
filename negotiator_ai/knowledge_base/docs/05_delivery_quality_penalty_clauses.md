# Delivery & Quality Penalty Clauses

## Why Penalty Clauses Are Negotiating Levers
Including delivery and quality SLAs with penalty clauses in your supply agreement:
1. Signals you are a serious, experienced buyer — shifts the power dynamic.
2. Provides real financial protection if the supplier underdelivers.
3. Creates a lever: *offer to remove a penalty clause in exchange for a price concession.*

## Standard Delivery SLA Structures

### On-Time Delivery (OTD) Clause
> "Supplier guarantees delivery within [X] business days of purchase order confirmation. Deliveries more than [2] business days late will incur a penalty of [1–2%] of the invoice value per day late, up to a maximum of [10%] of the total order value."

**Industry standard penalty rate:** 0.5–2% per day, capped at 10–15% of order value.

### Force Majeure Exceptions
Always include: supplier is not penalized for delays caused by events beyond their control (natural disasters, government restrictions, carrier failures outside supplier's control). This is fair and makes your clause non-negotiable for reasonable suppliers.

## Quality SLA Structures

### Defect Rate Clause
> "Supplier guarantees a defect rate of no more than [2%] of delivered units. If defect rate exceeds this threshold, supplier will replace defective units at no cost and issue a credit of [5%] of the affected order value."

**Industry benchmarks for defect thresholds:**
- Packaging materials: ≤2% acceptable defect rate
- Food ingredients: ≤1% (stricter due to safety requirements)
- Electronics/equipment: ≤0.5%
- General merchandise: ≤3%

### Right of Inspection Clause
> "Buyer reserves the right to inspect a sample of [10%] of delivered units within [5] business days of receipt. Supplier agrees to resolve any quality disputes within [10] business days."

## Penalty Clause as a Price Negotiation Tool

### The Trade-off Tactic
Use the penalty clause as currency in price negotiations:
> "We're requiring a delivery SLA with a 1% per day penalty clause. However, if you can meet our target price of [X], we're willing to reduce the penalty to 0.5% per day and extend the cure period to 3 days."

This creates a perceived concession from you that costs little but moves the price negotiation forward.

### The "Insurance Framing"
> "The penalty clause is our insurance against disruption costs — re-ordering, production downtime, customer delays. We're happy to keep it light if we can have confidence in the price."

## Supplier Pushback and Responses

**Pushback:** "We've never had penalty clauses with our customers."
**Response:** "That's helpful context. We find that documenting service expectations protects both sides — it aligns our expectations upfront and avoids disputes later. Many of our other suppliers have found it straightforward."

**Pushback:** "We can't commit to a 2-day window — lead times vary."
**Response:** "Understood. What lead time CAN you commit to? We're flexible on the window if the commitment is clear."

## Key Benchmarks
- % of SME supply agreements that include OTD SLA: ~40% (underpenetrated — use this as a signal of professionalism)
- Typical OTD penalty: **0.5–2% per day, capped at 10%** of order value
- Defect replacement + credit combination: **5% credit + free replacement** is standard
- Time-to-resolve quality disputes: **7–14 business days** (industry norm)

## Template Language
```
DELIVERY SLA: Supplier shall deliver goods within [X] business days of PO confirmation.
For each day of delay beyond the agreed window (excluding force majeure events), 
Supplier shall credit Buyer [1%] of the invoice value, up to a maximum of [10%].

QUALITY GUARANTEE: Supplier warrants a defect rate ≤ [2%] of shipped units.
If defect rate exceeds this threshold in any single shipment, Supplier shall 
replace defective units within [10] business days at no additional cost.
```
