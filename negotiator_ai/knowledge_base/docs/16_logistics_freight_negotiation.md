# Logistics, Freight & Delivery Negotiation

## Overview
Logistics and freight costs are negotiable far more than most small businesses realize. Whether you're buying inbound freight from a supplier or managing your own outbound logistics, significant savings are available through structured negotiation.

## Inbound Freight (Supplier-Delivered Goods)

### 1. Free-on-Board (FOB) Negotiation
FOB determines who pays freight and bears risk:
- **FOB Origin:** You pay freight and bear risk from supplier's dock. Use when you have cheaper freight options.
- **FOB Destination:** Supplier pays freight to your door. Better for small businesses without logistics infrastructure.

**Tactic:** If a supplier quotes FOB Origin, ask for FOB Destination — freight is often their negotiating concession:
> "Can we do FOB Destination? If you include freight in the price, we can simplify billing and avoid coordination costs on our side."

### 2. Freight-Free Threshold Negotiation
Negotiate a minimum order size at which delivery is free:
> "Our current order size is [$X]. What's the order value at which you'd include freight? Let's work toward that threshold."

Many suppliers have a freight-free threshold they'll share if asked. If yours is just below it, rounding up often saves more than the freight cost.

### 3. Consolidated Delivery Schedules
More deliveries = more cost for the supplier. Reducing delivery frequency saves them money that you can negotiate back:
> "We're willing to move from weekly to bi-weekly deliveries. What's the freight saving for you, and can we split it?"

### 4. Return/Reverse Logistics Costs
Negotiate free return shipping for defective or overshipped goods:
> "For any returns due to quality issues or shipping errors on your side, we expect free return freight and a replacement within [X] days."

## Third-Party Logistics (3PL) Negotiation

### Volume Commitment for Rate Reduction
3PL providers (courier, freight forwarders) offer significant rate reductions for committed volumes:
- 200+ shipments/month: **10–15% below spot rate**
- 500+ shipments/month: **15–25% below spot rate**
- Annual contract with volume minimum: **20–30% below spot rate**

### Negotiate Rate Cards, Not Per-Shipment Rates
Always negotiate a rate card (fixed prices per weight/zone) rather than letting the provider quote per shipment:
> "We'd like a 12-month rate card at [X shipments/month]. Zone-wise rates, with a fuel surcharge cap of [Y%]."

### Fuel Surcharge Negotiation
Fuel surcharges are a significant and volatile component of freight costs. Negotiate:
- A cap on fuel surcharge percentage (e.g., no more than 15% of base rate)
- A quarterly review mechanism linked to a fuel price index
- Elimination of fuel surcharge in exchange for a slightly higher base rate (if fuel prices are currently low)

### Multi-Carrier Strategy
Use at least 2 freight providers and route-optimize:
> "We work with [Carrier A] for local deliveries and we're evaluating [Carrier B] for interstate. We'd like you to sharpen your interstate rate — what's your best offer for 100+ shipments/month?"

## Freight Benchmark Rates (India SME Context)
| Route Type | Standard Rate | Negotiated Target (volume buyer) |
|---|---|---|
| Local (within city) | ₹50–150/kg | ₹30–80/kg |
| Regional (same state) | ₹25–60/kg | ₹15–35/kg |
| Interstate | ₹20–45/kg | ₹12–28/kg |
| Air freight (domestic) | ₹80–150/kg | ₹60–100/kg |

## Packaging-Freight Integration
Sometimes reducing product packaging size or weight reduces freight cost more than negotiating freight rates. Consider:
- Lighter packaging material (saves weight per shipment)
- Better packing density (fit more units per cubic meter)
- Discuss with freight provider: are you being charged by weight or volumetric weight?
