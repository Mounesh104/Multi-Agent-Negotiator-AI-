# Early Payment Discounts & Cash Flow Negotiation

## The Early Payment Discount as a Two-Way Lever
Early payment discounts (EPDs) benefit suppliers (they get cash faster) and can benefit you (you save on invoice value). Negotiating EPDs is valuable even if you rarely use them — they give you optionality.

## Standard EPD Structures

| Discount | Payment Window | Effective Annual Rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1/10 Net 30 | Pay in 10 days, save 1% | ~18.4% annualized |
| 2/10 Net 30 | Pay in 10 days, save 2% | ~36.7% annualized |
| 1/10 Net 60 | Pay in 10 days, save 1% | ~7.3% annualized |
| 2/10 Net 60 | Pay in 10 days, save 2% | ~14.7% annualized |

The annualized rate decreases as you extend the net payment window — making Net 60 + EPD a better deal than Net 30 + EPD if you have flexibility.

## How to Negotiate EPDs
1. **Ask first:** Most suppliers don't advertise EPDs but will offer them when asked.
2. **Frame it as mutual benefit:** You're providing cash flow certainty; they're sharing a portion of the financial benefit.
3. **Start at 2%, negotiate to 1.5%:** The supplier's cost of capital (or factoring cost) is usually the implicit ceiling on what they'll offer.

## Dynamic Discounting Programs
Larger suppliers sometimes offer **dynamic discounting** — a sliding scale where the discount is proportional to how early you pay (e.g., 0.05% per day paid early). Ask if they have such a program.

## Cash-Advance Lever (Supplier Financing)
Some small businesses negotiate **supplier financing** — where the supplier extends credit at below-market rates in exchange for exclusivity or volume commitment:
- Supplier agrees to 90-day payment terms with no interest if you maintain monthly order volume above a threshold.
- This is effectively a 90-day revolving credit facility from your supplier.
- Achievable with trusted suppliers after 12+ months of relationship.

## Invoice Factoring Context (for your negotiation)
If your supplier mentions they use invoice factoring (selling their receivables to a third party at a discount), they may actually *prefer* faster payment to avoid the factoring cost. Use this:
> "We understand many suppliers use factoring, which costs 2–4% of invoice value. We can offer earlier payment in exchange for passing some of that savings back to us — say 1.5% discount for 10-day payment."

## Red Lines: What NOT to Accept
- Payment terms shorter than Net 30 for ongoing supply relationships (except first order)
- Late fees that compound (should be simple interest, not compound)
- Mandatory upfront payment >30% on established relationships (>6 months)

## Benchmark Summary
- Average EPD offered in B2B SME supplier relationships: **1–2%** for 10-day payment
- % of suppliers willing to offer EPD when asked: **~60%** (most simply don't advertise it)
- Cash flow benefit of Net 60 vs. Net 30 on $20K/month spend: **$20,000 freed permanently**
