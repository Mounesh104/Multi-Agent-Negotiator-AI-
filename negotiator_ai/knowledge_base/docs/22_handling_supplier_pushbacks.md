# Negotiating with Supplier Resistance — Handling Common Pushbacks

## Overview
Even the best-prepared negotiator faces pushback. The key is to have prepared responses for the most common supplier objections. This document covers the 12 most common supplier resistance patterns and how to handle each.

## The 12 Common Pushbacks

### 1. "This is our best price."
**What it usually means:** "I don't want to negotiate" or "I don't have authority to go lower."
**Response:** "I appreciate that. Let's set price aside for a moment — what flexibility do you have on payment terms, delivery lead times, or minimum order quantities? Sometimes value comes from the full package, not just the unit price."

Alternatively: "Could you check with your manager? We're very close to being able to commit today."

### 2. "Our prices are fixed / We have standard pricing."
**Response:** "Standard pricing is for standard customers. We're offering a committed volume of [X], consistent orders, and on-time payment. That's worth a conversation about non-standard terms."

### 3. "The market price has gone up."
**Response:** "We understand market prices fluctuate. Can you share the specific input that's driven the increase? If it's [material X], we've seen that index and the increase looks smaller than what you're quoting. Let's look at the components."

If you don't have specifics: "We'd like to understand the cost structure. Can we see a cost breakdown?"

### 4. "Your quantity is too small for a discount."
**Response:** "We're starting small to test the relationship, but our plan is to grow to [Y units/month] within 3 months. Let's agree on a price that reflects where we're going, not just where we're starting. We can document the escalation commitment."

### 5. "We can't do Net 60 — we have cash flow requirements."
**Response:** "Understood. What if we offered Net 30 with ACH auto-pay, so you're guaranteed on-time payment with no collection risk? That's effectively better than Net 30 from an uncertainty standpoint. Can we get a rate benefit for that reliability?"

### 6. "We've had this price for all customers — I can't make an exception."
**Response:** "We're not asking for an exception — we're asking for a volume-appropriate price. Your other customers who order [X units/month] probably do pay differently than first-time single-order customers. Where do we fit?"

### 7. "Another supplier can't match our quality."
**Response:** "We value quality highly — that's why you're our first choice. But quality and price aren't mutually exclusive. At our target price, we'd still be working with you — we just need the price to reflect our business reality. What would it take to make that work?"

### 8. "I need to check with my management." (Stall tactic)
**Response:** "Of course. Let me give you something concrete to take back: we're ready to commit to [X units/month, Net 30, with a PO today] if we can agree on [$Y per unit]. If you can get approval for that, we can close today."

Attach a decision deadline: "We'd need to know by [date] to finalize our sourcing for Q[X]."

### 9. "We already gave you a discount from our list price."
**Response:** "We appreciate that — and we understand your list price is the starting point. We're comparing against market pricing for our volume and category, not against list. Where do you land vs. the market for a buyer of our size?"

### 10. "We'll lose money at that price."
**Response:** "We don't want anyone to lose money — that doesn't build a lasting relationship. Can you walk me through your cost structure? I'd like to understand where the floor is and make sure our target is above it."

This invitation to transparency often reveals there IS room — and the supplier's discomfort with sharing costs signals there's margin available.

### 11. "Take it or leave it."
**Response:** Silence. Then: "That's a strong statement. I'm not ready to leave it — but I'm also not able to take it at that price. Let me think about whether there's a version of this that works. Can we speak again [tomorrow / in two days]?"

Never respond to ultimatums immediately. Let 24 hours pass. Return with a small counter and let them decide.

### 12. "We have plenty of other buyers."
**Response:** "I'm sure you do — and we're glad you're in demand. But we're evaluating you because we think there's a good long-term fit here. We'd like to be one of your reliable, growing accounts. That's worth something — if you'd like to have that conversation."

## General Pushback Principles
- **Never respond to pushback immediately with a concession.** Pause, acknowledge, and redirect.
- **Show empathy, not sympathy.** "I understand that's your position" ≠ "I accept that's your position."
- **Use questions, not statements.** "What would make that work?" is harder to argue with than "that doesn't work for us."
- **Always keep the door open.** Even if you walk away, leave on terms that allow them to return with a better offer.
