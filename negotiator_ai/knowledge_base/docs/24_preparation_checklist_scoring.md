# Negotiation Preparation Checklist & Deal Scoring

## Pre-Negotiation Preparation (Do This Before Every Negotiation)

### Step 1: Know Your Numbers
- [ ] What is the supplier's quoted price?
- [ ] What is my target price? (researched, not guessed)
- [ ] What is my reservation price (walkaway point)?
- [ ] What is my opening anchor? (apply bracketing formula)
- [ ] What is the estimated savings if I hit my target? (annual/quarterly)

### Step 2: Know Your Leverage
- [ ] Am I a repeat customer? If yes: how many orders? Total spend?
- [ ] Do I have competing quotes? If yes: how many? What range?
- [ ] Am I a first-time buyer? (affects strategy significantly)
- [ ] What volume am I offering? Is there room to increase as a concession?
- [ ] What is my urgency level? (never reveal if high — creates vulnerability)
- [ ] What is my BATNA? Is it real and ready?

### Step 3: Know the Supplier
- [ ] How dependent are they on my business (% of their revenue)?
- [ ] Are they in a busy or slow period?
- [ ] Have there been any quality or delivery issues? (leverage in negotiations)
- [ ] Do I know who has authority to approve discounts? (go above the rep if needed)
- [ ] What is their likely BATNA? (do they have other buyers for my volume?)

### Step 4: Plan Your Concession Inventory
- [ ] List all non-price concessions I can offer (ranked by cost to me)
- [ ] Identify 2–3 "high-value to them / low-cost to me" concessions for trading
- [ ] Identify my real price limit (do not give this away)

### Step 5: Prepare Your Scripts
- [ ] Opening statement (confident, researched, non-apologetic)
- [ ] Anchor delivery (precise number, brief justification, then silence)
- [ ] Response to "That's our best price" (have it ready)
- [ ] Response to "Our costs have gone up" (have it ready)
- [ ] Closing statement (trial close, assumptive close)

## Deal Scoring Rubric
Use this rubric to score any negotiation outcome (1–5 scale):

### Price Achievement
| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | Achieved better than target price |
| 4 | Achieved target price exactly |
| 3 | Within 3% of target price |
| 2 | 3–7% above target price |
| 1 | More than 7% above target (near original quote) |

### Terms Achievement
| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | Got better terms than asked (Net 60+ or EPD + delivery free) |
| 4 | Got terms as asked |
| 3 | Got partial terms (e.g., Net 45 when asked for Net 60) |
| 2 | Minimal improvement (Net 30 when standard) |
| 1 | No terms improvement from original quote |

### Relationship Quality Post-Negotiation
| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | Supplier expressed enthusiasm, proactively offered future benefits |
| 4 | Positive close, good tone, clear path forward |
| 3 | Neutral — deal done, no warmth or friction |
| 2 | Mild tension — deal done but strained |
| 1 | Hostile close — relationship damaged |

### Process Quality
| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 5 | Stayed composed, used all planned tactics, no improvised concessions |
| 4 | Used most planned tactics, minor deviations |
| 3 | Used some tactics, missed some planned moves |
| 2 | Improvised significantly, deviated from plan |
| 1 | Panicked, conceded too quickly, anchor not used |

### Composite Score Interpretation
- **18–20:** Excellent negotiation — strong outcome and strong process
- **14–17:** Good negotiation — solid outcome with room for process improvement
- **10–13:** Average — some value captured but significant room left on the table
- **6–9:** Poor outcome — revisit preparation and tactics before next negotiation
- **Below 6:** Learning experience — study core frameworks and try again

## Post-Negotiation Review
After every negotiation, spend 5 minutes answering:
1. Did I achieve my target price? If not, why not?
2. Which tactic worked best?
3. Which tactic didn't work — and why?
4. What would I do differently?
5. What is the supplier's pressure point I should remember for next time?

Record these in your negotiation history (the system will store them for you).
